# Printed Index

The design-system board behaves like a printer's specimen index rather than a software dashboard. Warm paper remains visible between every sample, while black rules, restrained labels, and exact registration marks make the page feel measured and physical.

Color is evidence, not decoration. Every ink appears as a solid printed field with its identifier and value attached. Cobalt and terracotta recur as navigational accents so the many samples still read as one coherent object.

Typography demonstrates hierarchy through actual scale contrast. Serif, grotesk, condensed, and monospaced voices are shown performing their intended roles instead of being described in paragraphs. Labels stay quiet and precise.

Composition is taught through miniature posters. Each thumbnail exposes subject mass, paper exposure, title collision, and alignment anchors. The carrier section then shifts from abstract rectangles to recognizable physical silhouettes, making application differences visible at a glance.

The board should feel patiently assembled, with consistent baselines, optical spacing, and no ornamental effects. Its purpose is rapid visual recall: a human chooses by looking, while the matching IDs let the generation system make the same choice programmatically.
